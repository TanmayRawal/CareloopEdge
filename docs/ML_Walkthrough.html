How the AI reads a brain MRI study

From public datasets to a signed INT8 model on a Raspberry Pi 4: what the model is, how it was trained and tested, how it explains itself, and which checks stop it from guessing. Every code excerpt below is from the project repository.

classes glioma · meningioma · pituitary · no tumour backbone ResNet-50 runtime ONNX INT8, Pi 4 CPU macro-F1 0.98 on held-out studies

01Overview

The AI looks at a patient's whole brain MRI study and suggests one of four answers. It also shows the doctor where on the scan its evidence came from. Everything runs on the Pi 4's CPU, with no cloud and no GPU. The AI only suggests: a doctor reviews and signs every result, and patients never see the AI output.

The code is split into a shared library used by both training and the device (neuroedge/), the training scripts (training/) and the device engine (edge/engine.py).

We found leaks in the public benchmarks

In BRISC's official split, 566 of the 1,000 test images are copies of training images, and 462 of those are mirrored left to right. A score on that split is inflated. BDNeuro, described as independent hospital data, turned out to be 3,768 of 5,941 copies of BRISC.

  • Images were compared with flip-aware correlation on 64×64 thumbnails. The copy threshold (0.98) sits in an empty gap of the score distribution.
  • All data was pooled and re-split by patient group, so no patient is in two splits: train 3,766, val 765, cal 670, test 799.
  • Only the 1,674 clean BDNeuro images were kept for the external test.
Four splits, four jobs. Train fits the weights. Val picks the best epoch and settings. Cal is used only to tune the safety thresholds (temperature, confidence sets, unusual-image detector). Test is opened once at the end.
The "scan type" shortcut. BRISC's no-tumour images mix CT, FLAIR, T2 and plain T1, while every tumour image is contrast-enhanced T1. A model could learn "which scan type is this?" instead of "is there a tumour?". v2 counters this with tumour-free slices taken from the same 3D scans as the tumour slices.

03Model architecture

One shared backbone with two heads (a multi-task network), defined in neuroedge/models.py.

Backbone

ResNet-50, ImageNet-pretrained, output stride 8. A 224×224 slice becomes a 28×28 grid of 2,048-feature vectors. The finer grid gives a sharper heatmap than the usual 7×7.

Segmentation head

A small FPN that combines 4 backbone levels into a pixel-level tumour mask. Trained on expert masks only.

Classification head

Lesion-attention pooling, then dropout and one linear layer giving the 4 class scores.

Lesion-attention pooling

A normal classifier averages all 784 grid cells equally. Ours weights each cell by how much tumour the segmentation head sees there, so the classifier mostly reads the tumour region. With no lesion the weights are equal and it falls back to a plain average.

The .detach() is deliberate: the classifier cannot reshape the mask to suit itself. The mask is learned only from the radiologists' annotations. The forward pass then pools the feature map with these weights:

Why this matters later. The class score is y_c = b_c + Σ a_ij · (W_c · A_ij): an exact sum of one contribution per cell. That sum is the heatmap, so the explanation is exact rather than approximate (section 07).

04Training

  • Loss: cross-entropy for the class, plus focal BCE and Dice for the mask. The mask loss applies only where a mask exists; no-tumour images count as empty masks. Deep supervision adds a mask loss at the coarsest level.
  • Optimiser: AdamW with a lower learning rate for the pretrained backbone than for the new heads, 2 warm-up epochs, then a cosine schedule.
  • Stability: EMA weights, bf16 mixed precision, early stopping on validation.
  • Settings choice: output stride {16, 8} × ε {0.005, 0.02, 0.05}, a pre-registered ablation judged on validation only.

05v1 to v2: the doctor's feedback

v1 classified one 2D slice. Our radiologist partner, Dr. Madhur Saxena, pointed out that no radiologist confirms a tumour from one image: they scroll through the whole study in all three planes.

v2 keeps the same architecture, preprocessing and training recipe. Only the data changed: slices from real 3D patient studies were added, in three planes, from train, val and cal patients only. Keeping everything else fixed isolates the effect of the new data. The targets below were written down before testing.

06Whole-study inference

Loading and checking the upload

neuroedge/volume.py reads a NIfTI file or a DICOM series. It drops identifying DICOM fields, reorients the volume to a standard orientation (RAS+), scales intensities once per volume with robust percentiles, and resamples thick slices to square pixels. Size limits block decompression-bomb files. Mixed uploads are refused:

Choosing 36 slices

Combining 36 answers into one

Most slices of a tumour patient show no tumour. A plain average over all 36 would bury a small tumour that appears on only a few slices. So the product averages the 5 slices most suggestive of tumour. This rule was chosen from four candidates (mean, top-k, max, lesion-weighted) on calibration patients only.

07Explainability

The doctor sees two things on the 3 key slices: a Grad-CAM heatmap and the lesion outline from the segmentation head, with its position and area in mm.

Because the class score is a sum of per-cell contributions, the heatmap is that sum. The engine checks this on every scan: the cells must add back up to the score.

On the deployed model this error is about 1e-7, because the pooling and the final layer were kept in FP32 during quantisation (section 09).

How to read the map. Its overall shape partly comes from the segmentation attention. After the classifier weights are randomised, the ranking of cells is destroyed (Spearman −0.04), but the envelope stays similar (SSIM 0.81). We describe it as lesion-weighted classifier evidence.

08Uncertainty and safety

All of these are fitted on the cal split and on the deployed INT8 model. When they were fitted on the PyTorch model instead, the unusual-image detector flagged 100% of normal images.

LayerWhat it does
Temperature scaling (T = 2.56)Makes the probabilities honest. Calibration error fell from 0.042 to 0.024.
Conformal 95% prediction set, per classReturns the set of plausible classes. More than one means uncertain, none means nothing fits; both go to review.
Mahalanobis detectorCompares the model's internal features with the training data. Warns above the 95% level, rejects above 99.5%.

09Running on the Pi 4

  • Export: PyTorch to ONNX (opset 17), then static INT8 quantisation with ONNX Runtime. Size falls from 96.6 MB to 24.7 MB.
  • Accuracy kept: INT8 is within 1 point of FP32 (McNemar p = 1.0, no significant difference).
  • Speed: a whole study (36 slices plus overlays) takes about 43–51 s on the Pi 4 CPU.

10Secure model delivery

The model travels from our PC to each clinic device through a registry, and is never stored unencrypted on the device.

If the signature check fails, the model does not run. On a healthy device the sync tool reports mri: version 0.3.0 verified OK.

12Limitations

  • One training seed; two more are still to be trained.
  • Test patients come from the same datasets as training patients. The external whole-study test on BraTS-Africa is pending.
  • IXI healthy scans are non-contrast T1, so the scan-type shortcut is reduced, not proven gone.
  • The 95% set is calibrated on slices, not patients. On the 2D test set it covered 91%, below the 93% target.
  • BraTS data is CC BY-NC: research and non-commercial use only.
  • Out of scope: CT, children, post-operative scans, tumour types outside the four classes (for example metastases).

13What is not ML

CareLoop's follow-up risk score is rule-based on purpose. It is a fixed, documented weighted sum of operational signals: days overdue, missed appointments, unanswered calls, contact details not working, an open referral, travel distance, and a booked appointment (which lowers it). High is 60 or more, Medium 30 or more.

It never uses the diagnosis, the report or any AI output, so it cannot carry over bias from the classifier, and it only orders the coordinator's list. A learned model would need real follow-up outcomes from a pilot, which is future work.
